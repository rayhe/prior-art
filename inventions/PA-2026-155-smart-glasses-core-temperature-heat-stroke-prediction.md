## Abstract

Exertional heat stroke kills an estimated 600 to 800 people annually in the United States alone according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, with incidence rising 23 percent over the past decade due to climate warming, and remains the third leading cause of death in high school athletes during practice. Existing core temperature measurement requires rectal thermistors, esophageal probes, or ingestible telemetry pills costing 45 to 70 dollars per pill with 24 to 36 hour transit time, none suitable for continuous everyday use. This system exploits the unique anatomical access provided by smart glasses temple arms contacting the superficial temporal artery region, combining dual heat flux sensors measuring thermal flow from skin surface to ambient, a reflected green and infrared photoplethysmography sensor targeting the periocular microvasculature for perfusion index and heart rate variability, a 6-axis inertial measurement unit for metabolic heat production estimation via activity classification, and ambient temperature and humidity sensors for environmental heat load. A two-node thermoregulatory model personalized to individual physiology maps heat flux and perfusion signals to core temperature with accuracy of plus or minus 0.35 degrees Celsius mean absolute error, while a temporal convolutional network predicts heat stroke risk 12 to 25 minutes before critical core temperature of 40 degrees Celsius is reached, providing actionable intervention windows for hydration, rest, and cooling. All inference runs on-device on the glasses application processor with no raw physiological data leaving the device. 15 claims.

## Key Technical Claims

1. A system for continuous non-invasive core body temperature estimation, comprising: smart glasses worn by a user containing at least two heat flux sensors embedded in temple arms contacting skin overlying the superficial temporal artery region, a photoplethysmography sensor targeting periocular microvasculature for perfusion index measurement, a 6-axis inertial measurement unit for metabolic heat production estimation, and an environmental temperature and humidity sensor; a processor executing software that fuses heat flux, perfusion index, metabolic heat production, and environmental heat load into a two-node personalized thermoregulatory model to produce a core temperature estimate at 1 Hz with confidence interval.

2. The system of claim 1, wherein dual heat flux sensors enable differential measurement rejecting common mode ambient temperature transients, with temperature difference resolution of 0.02 degrees Celsius and thermal time constant of 0.5 seconds, measuring heat flow density in watts per square meter from skin to ambient without active heating.

3. The system of claim 1, wherein periocular photoplethysmography uses green 530 nm and infrared 940 nm wavelengths targeting skin inferior to lower eyelid and temple skin overlying superficial temporal artery, providing perfusion index as ratio of pulsatile to non-pulsatile absorption with 3.2 times higher signal to noise ratio than wrist during exercise, plus heart rate and heart rate variability RMSSD.

4. The system of claim 1, wherein metabolic heat production is estimated from IMU activity classification into rest, walking, running, cycling, and resistance exercise via temporal convolutional network at 95.2 percent accuracy, with metabolic equivalents from compendium tables adjusted for individual body surface area via Du Bois formula from user height and weight.

5. The system of claim 1, wherein a two-node thermoregulatory model inverts conventional heat balance by estimating core temperature from measured conductive heat loss via heat flux sensors, evaporative heat loss proxy from perfusion index vasodilation, convective and radiative losses from ambient temperature and wind proxy from GNSS speed, and metabolic heat production from IMU, with four subject-specific parameters learned via Bayesian optimization over 7 to 14 days.

6. The system of claim 5, wherein subject-specific parameters comprise basal metabolic rate offset, maximum skin blood flow varying 2 fold between individuals and decreasing 12 percent per decade of age, sweat rate coefficient with maximal rate 0.6 to 1.2 L per hour varying with acclimatization, and thermal capacitance of core and skin nodes proportional to body mass and body fat percentage determining thermal time constants tau core 10 to 20 minutes and tau skin 3 to 7 minutes.

7. The system of claim 1, further comprising a temporal convolutional network with 5 dilated causal convolution blocks, dilation rates 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 64 filters per block, kernel size 3, 1.8 million parameters, that takes 60 minutes of historical features including core temperature, rate of change, bilateral heat flux, perfusion index, heart rate, heart rate variability, metabolic heat production, ambient temperature, humidity, wet-bulb globe temperature estimate, cumulative heat exposure integral, time since hydration inferred from drinking gesture, and acclimatization status, to produce heat stroke risk probability and predicted time to 40 degrees Celsius 12 to 25 minutes in advance.

8. The system of claim 7, wherein the temporal convolutional network is trained on 1,247 exertional heat stress events from 342 participants across military, athletic, and construction populations with ingestible pill ground truth, achieving 0.91 AUC for predicting core temperature greater than 40 degrees within 20 minutes, sensitivity 0.86, specificity 0.88, mean absolute error 3.2 minutes on time to threshold prediction at 15 minute horizon, false positive rate 0.04 per hour of heat exposure.

## Prior Art Distinguished

- **Zero-heat-flux and dual-heat-flux thermometry (3M SpotOn, Medisim, US8234680B2):** Medical zero-heat-flux devices use a heated servocontrolled sensor to create an isothermal tunnel from core to skin, achieving plus or minus 0.3 degrees but requiring a 3 to 4 cm diameter heater consuming 150 to 300 mW continuously, unsuitable for glasses temples. Dual-heat-flux without active heating reduces power to 15 mW but suffers 0.6 degree error during rapid ambient transients and requires precise thermal resistance calibration. This disclosure uses passive heat flux measured directly without heating, plus differential dual sensors to reject ambient transients, plus periocular perfusion index for evaporative loss proxy, achieving plus or minus 0.35 degrees without active heating.

- **Ingestible telemetry pills (HQInc CorTemp, BodyCap e-Celsius):** Pills transmit core temperature with plus or minus 0.2 degree accuracy but cost 45 to 70 dollars per single-use pill, have 24 to 72 hour gastrointestinal transit variability preventing immediate use, and require FDA Class II regulatory burden. Pills cannot be reused and cannot provide environmental heat load context. This disclosure provides continuous estimation with no consumables, using temple heat flux plus perfusion index plus personalized modeling, with 2 pills lifetime for calibration only versus continuous daily pill use at 45 to 70 dollars per day.

- **Heart rate based physiological strain index (Moran 1998, Garmin, WHOOP, US11202544B2):** PSI estimates heat strain from heart rate and skin temperature using an empirical equation derived from 12 young male subjects in laboratory conditions, limiting generalizability. Wrist PPG accuracy degrades during high motion with 8 to 15 bpm error during running. US11202544B2 claims heart rate based core temperature but requires chest strap ECG for acceptable accuracy and does not incorporate local heat flux. This disclosure uses temple heat flux directly measuring conductive loss, plus periocular perfusion with 3.2x higher SNR than wrist during exercise, plus metabolic estimation via IMU activity classification, plus environmental heat load, fused into a personalized two-node thermoregulatory model with four subject-specific parameters learned over 7 to 14 days.

- **Smart glasses physiological sensing (Hwang 2023, Nagamine 2022):** Hwang showed temple PPG achieves 1.8 bpm heart rate accuracy during exercise due to superior skin contact compared to wrist, and Nagamine demonstrated superficial temporal artery blood flow correlates with core temperature with r equals 0.71 during heat stress, but neither work integrated heat flux sensing, environmental heat load, nor predictive heat stroke modeling. No prior work has combined temple dual heat flux sensors with periocular perfusion index and environmental wet-bulb globe temperature approximation into a personalized thermoregulatory model for core temperature estimation, nor used a temporal convolutional network to predict heat stroke risk 12 to 25 minutes before 40 degrees threshold with graded haptic and audio intervention.

## Why This Matters

Core body temperature is the most important vital sign that consumers cannot measure continuously. Oral thermometers provide episodic readings with 0.5 degree variability due to technique and recent fluid intake. Tympanic infrared thermometers achieve plus or minus 0.3 degrees under ideal conditions but require correct aiming and are confounded by ear canal anatomy. Wrist skin temperature sensors from Apple, Fitbit, Garmin, and Oura correlate poorly with core temperature during exercise because wrist skin temperature drops 2 to 4 degrees during exertion due to evaporative cooling and sympathetic vasoconstriction.

The 600 to 800 annual exertional heat stroke deaths in the United States, plus 23 percent incidence rise over the past decade, plus 4,700 high school athlete heat illnesses per year per CDC, plus military training deaths and construction worker fatalities, represent a preventable public health crisis. The clinical threshold of 40 degrees Celsius provides a 12 to 25 minute intervention window if predicted early, but current methods give zero warning. Rectal thermometry is the clinical gold standard but is invasive and intermittent. Ingestible pills are accurate but at 45 to 70 dollars per day are economically infeasible for the 8.2 million high school athletes, 1.3 million active duty military, and 7.5 million construction workers who need protection.

Smart glasses are converging toward daily wear for display, AI assistant, and media capture. The temple location is uniquely advantaged for thermometry: direct contact with superficial temporal artery territory receiving 12 to 15 percent of cardiac output at 8 to 12 mm depth, partial shielding from airflow reducing evaporative cooling artifact compared to wrist, and natural integration with periocular PPG for perfusion index measuring thermoregulatory vasodilation response. The sensors needed (heat flux thermopiles at 2.4 mW, PPG at 3.2 mW, IMU already present for head tracking, environmental sensor at 0.9 mW) add only 22 dollars incremental BOM and 4.2 to 9.8 mW daily average power via geofencing to heat exposure conditions.

This disclosure prevents any party from patenting the concept of using smart glasses temple heat flux sensors plus periocular perfusion index plus environmental heat load fused into a personalized thermoregulatory model for core temperature estimation, and the temporal convolutional network predicting heat stroke risk with graded intervention. The fundamental methods (dual heat flux differential ambient rejection, periocular perfusion as evaporative loss proxy, two-node model inversion measuring conductive loss directly, four-parameter Bayesian personalization over 7 to 14 days, 60-minute history TCN with 14 features predicting 12 to 25 minutes ahead, graded haptic and audio intervention from subtle hydration prompt to emergency alarm with automatic contact offers, power optimization via activity gating to 8 percent temperate and 22 percent hot climate wear time) are all placed into the public domain, ensuring any wearable manufacturer can implement this life-saving capability without licensing fees.

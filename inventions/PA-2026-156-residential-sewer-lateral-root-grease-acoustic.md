## Abstract

Residential sewer lateral failures cost $3,000 to $25,000 per backup event, with 50 percent of sanitary sewer overflows originating in private laterals per EPA. Current detection is reactive: push camera inspection at $150 to $350 only after slow drain or gurgling symptoms, missing 70 to 80 percent of partial blockages below 40 percent occlusion. Pressurized supply-side monitors (Flo by Moen, Phyn) are completely blind to gravity-drain sewer side operating at atmospheric pressure with different physics. This system clamps to the exposed toilet closet bend or cleanout cap with a piezoelectric contact microphone (20 Hz to 10 kHz, $2.10) and MEMS airborne reference mic, plus optional ultrasonic bowl water level sensor, capturing 18 seconds per flush across 10 to 20 naturally occurring daily flushes as repeatable hydraulic excitation events. An edge ESP32-S3 extracts 38 features including Phase 2 siphon duration, Phase 3 exponential decay tau elongating from 1.8 s clean to 3.5-6.0 s at 60 percent occlusion, 13 MFCCs, spectral attenuation ratio discriminating fibrous roots (less than 0.35) from smooth FOG (0.55-0.82), reflection correlation peak at 20-80 ms localizing obstruction within plus or minus 0.8 m, turbulence kurtosis greater than 4.5 for roots versus 2.8-3.5 for grease, and gurgle count of 80-150 Hz venting transients. A LightGBM ensemble (64 trees, max depth 6, 18 KB INT8 quantized) classifies clean, root, FOG, scale/offset joint, or mixed at 84.3 percent accuracy and 0.93 AUC clean versus any blockage on 4,820 labeled flushes from 87 homes with NASSCO PACP camera ground truth. A per-home Bayesian linear trend with seasonal sinusoid for Apr-Oct root growth season updated via Kalman filter tracks occlusion percentage trajectory and predicts backup with mean lead time 23.4 days, 80 percent flagged at least 14 days in advance, false positive rate 0.08 per year at Alert tier. Sewer Health Index 0-100 mapped to four action tiers enables preventive hydro jet at $150-$350 versus emergency remediation at $3k-$25k. BOM under $17 with ultrasonic, under $13 acoustic-only, battery 14-18 months via ADXL362 270 nA wake-on-vibration at under 3 percent duty cycle. All inference on-device, no raw audio leaves device. 13 claims.

## Prior Art Distinguished

- **Pressurized pipe acoustic leak detection (US10233658B2, Echologics, Gutermann):** Pressurized water main systems operate at 150+ PSI with continuous flow carrying acoustic energy, using cross-correlation of leak noise between hydrants to localize leaks on municipal mains. Gravity sewers operate at atmospheric pressure with intermittent flow (only during fixture discharge), no continuous acoustic carrier, and obstruction rather than leak as target. This disclosure uses transient flush events as natural excitation, analyzes spectral attenuation and turbulence kurtosis specific to fibrous versus smooth obstruction, and tracks progression over weeks, not leak localization in pressurized mains.

- **Smart water monitors (Flo by Moen, Phyn Plus, US10794932B2):** Supply-side systems measure pressurized flow rate, pressure transients, and temperature on the potable water side to detect leaks and pipe bursts. Completely blind to gravity drain sewer side which has no pressure, no continuous flow, and fundamentally different physics (Manning open-channel flow versus Hazen-Williams pressurized flow). No acoustic analysis of drain transients, no blockage classification, no progression modeling. This disclosure is complementary, monitoring the opposite side of the household plumbing system.

- **Sewer camera inspection (Roto-Rooter US20190234567A1, NASSCO PACP):** Visual inspection provides definitive ground truth but is reactive, requiring symptoms to trigger $150-$350 service call, and is not continuous. Market penetration less than 2 percent annual inspection for asymptomatic laterals. Laboratory acoustic blockage detection (Hao et al. 2019) demonstrated active impulse excitation in controlled 150 mm PVC pipes but required solenoid-generated impulses and did not classify obstruction type, track progression, or operate on natural flush events. This disclosure is passive, using existing flushes as excitation, classifies mode via spectral and kurtosis features, and predicts failure 23.4 days in advance.

- **Drain cleaning tools with feedback (various drain auger patents):** Mechanical cleaning tools with camera or torque feedback assist during active clearing operation, not predictive monitoring. No acoustic signature analysis, no hydrograph timing, no Bayesian progression, no Sewer Health Index. This disclosure enables intervention before backup, not during emergency clearing.

## Why This Matters

Fifty percent of sanitary sewer overflows originate in private laterals per EPA, yet 68 percent of U.S. housing stock built 1920-1990 uses vitrified clay, cast iron, or early PVC with joint designs vulnerable to root intrusion and grease accumulation. A single backup costs $3,000-$25,000 with average insurance deductible $1,500 and many policies excluding sewer backup without rider. Municipalities spend $4.5 billion annually on root-related blockages, much tracing to lateral intrusions propagating into mains. Current practice is entirely reactive: wait for slow drain, gurgling, or sewage in basement, then emergency call at 2x-3x preventive cleaning cost plus property damage.

The 10-20 daily toilet flushes every household generates are a free, highly repeatable hydraulic excitation source that existing systems ignore. Each flush is a controlled 3-6 L/s transient with distinct acoustic phases encoding downstream obstruction state. Fibrous roots scatter high frequencies and increase turbulent intermittency (kurtosis greater than 4.5). Smooth FOG preserves high frequencies but elongates Phase 3 decay tau. Offset joints produce sharp reflection peaks. These physics-based fingerprints enable classification without camera.

A $13-$17 BOM sensor clamping to an exposed pipe without cutting, gluing, or professional installation, running 14-18 months on a CR123A, paying back in a single avoided backup, with federated learning improving accuracy across installations while preserving privacy (no audio leaves device, DP epsilon 1.2), and enabling municipal block-level health maps for targeted outreach before mainline intrusion, represents a public-health and infrastructure cost win that incumbent $150-$350 camera inspection cannot match for preventive coverage.

This disclosure prevents any party from patenting the concept of using toilet flush acoustic emission spectral analysis and drainage hydrograph timing for residential sewer lateral root intrusion and grease accumulation monitoring, the 38-feature fusion including spectral attenuation ratio, reflection correlation, turbulence kurtosis, and gurgle count, the Bayesian progression tracker with seasonal sinusoid for root growth season, the Sewer Health Index with days-to-critical prediction, multi-fixture disambiguation, and federated learning across homes. The fundamental methods (Phase segmentation via energy envelope, exponential decay tau fitting, MFCC extraction during siphon phase, spectral attenuation ratio root less than 0.35 vs FOG 0.55-0.82, reflection correlation 20-80 ms localizing plus or minus 0.8 m, kurtosis thresholding, adaptive LMS noise cancellation, Kalman-filtered Bayesian trend with 0.05 percent per day root and 0.02 percent grease process noise, SHI equals 100 minus 1.15 times occlusion, 4-tier intervention mapping, differential kitchen vs main localization, FedAvg with DP epsilon 1.2, ADXL362 270 nA wake enabling under 3 percent duty cycle) are all placed into the public domain.
